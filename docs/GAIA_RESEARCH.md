## Supported directions

**Gaia frames and distortion checks.** [Libralato, Bedin & Burgasser, ARES](https://app.cochat.ai/from/chatgpt/fetch/papers/10.1051/0004-6361/202557737), with the additional [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08533), uses ePSFs on native images, propagated Gaia positions, and geometric-distortion checks; its error budget includes both the target source and the transformation. This supports separate checks for local translation/affine transformations and covariance calibration. Do not transfer HST accuracy from the paper to SPHEREx. The pipeline already performs Gaia/CV steps; color, PSF, and noise differences from HST still need their own measurements.

**Address blending before increasing confidence.** The [official SPHEREx pipeline source](https://github.com/Caltech-IPAC/spherex-pipelines) uses Tractor and the `tractor-spherex` fork. This suggests prioritizing a multi-source forward model on native pixels instead of only subtracting templates or raising score thresholds. This is a team development proposal based on referenced sources and injection results, not a Tractor module already integrated into the demo. Check the license, input/PSF structure, and dependency scope before selecting software.

**Vision models for image sequences have a real example.** [Meisner et al. 2026, SMDET-1](https://app.cochat.ai/from/chatgpt/fetch/papers/10.3847/1538-3881/ae8e73) describes moving-source detection with a recurrent CNN on unWISE time series, training on artificial sources, and visual review of highly ranked candidates. The paper also shows the role of blended noise and verification with higher-resolution data. This direction fits the goal of “highlight, then send for human review.” However, multi-epoch WISE W1/W2 differs from SPHEREx, whose PSF and spectrum vary; a model trained on WISE cannot be considered validated for SPHEREx.

## Application to the current product

1. **Already implemented:** open a 0.32° field, apply Gaia quality gates, propagate covariance, split training/held-out stars, test changes to the star sample and leave-one-star-out behavior, and cache centroids to avoid repeating about 1,700 stellar fits when only reporting/verification changes.
2. **Next priority:** jointly fit the target and background sources, and check the full spectral response. Injections currently show background bias larger than some conditional errors; adding a CNN now would not address that cause.
3. **Benchmark before modeling:** use masks, variance, PSF, wavelength/bandwidth, and image sequences. Inject a range of speeds/directions/brightnesses in separated sky regions; split train/validation/test by region and image provenance, not randomly among variants on the same background. Retain stationary sources, real backgrounds, and crowded fields as negative controls; report precision/recovery by brightness and displacement.
4. **Review flow:** the model produces scores/segmentation to prioritize regions for inspection; evidence still includes original images, masks, epochs, spectrum, centroid/covariance, and provenance. Compare the vision model with image-processing baselines; a human reviewer makes the decision. No model has been trained or run for inference in this version.
5. **Web efficiency:** continue scientific preprocessing in Python, cache by hash, and serve only small products to the browser. Choose browser-side or backend inference after benchmarking accuracy/latency/memory; do not trade scientific error for a faster demo.

Schema and calibration were read directly from [Gaia DR3 at AIP/DPAC](https://gaia.aip.de/metadata/gaiadr3/gaia_source/), [IRSA data products/ePSF](https://caltech-ipac.github.io/spherex-archive-documentation/spherex-data-products/), and the [cutout API](https://caltech-ipac.github.io/spherex-archive-documentation/spherex-data-access/). Quality cuts in code are declared settings for this experiment, not a guarantee that every source passing them is unblended or single.

Hypothesis to test: an extremely red SED, wavelength gradient across pixels, and detector orientation may affect photocenters differently from the Gaia-star sample. This is not a verified cause of the vector discrepancy; test residuals by color, wavelength, and orientation in a multi-source model.

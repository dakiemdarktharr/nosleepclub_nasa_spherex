# SPHEREx Reference Views

Sources from NASA, JPL, Caltech, and BAE Systems were reviewed on 14 November 2026. The supplied image matches the orbital illustration **PIA26531**, with the spacecraft above Earth’s limb and a view of the three shield rims and the back of the solar panel. Use this composition for the opening screen. Use the 2024–2025 integration photos and NASA Eyes model to review other angles. [NASA Photojournal PIA26531](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/spherex-in-orbit-artists-concept/)

## 3D asset and verification

The web asset **[spherex.glb](../web/assets/mission/spherex.glb)** is **3,829,608 bytes** and packages a glTF model with five textures downloaded by the public NASA Eyes viewer. Geometry, component transforms, and material definitions follow the source. Three 4096 × 4096 textures were reduced to 2048 × 2048 to lower memory use; two 512 × 512 textures retain their original bytes. The file requires `EXT_texture_webp` and `KHR_texture_transform`. All textures are embedded in the GLB. [Source glTF model](https://eyes.nasa.gov/assets/static/models/sc_spherex/spherex.gltf) · [Provenance and SHA-256](../web/assets/mission/spherex.provenance.json)

Blender 5.2 imported **55 meshes, 75,425 triangles, and 5 textures**. Rendered views covered the shield opening, underside, and both faces of the solar panel. In the glTF coordinate system, the shield opening faces **+Y** and the panel is on **+X**. The bounding-box center is approximately `[0.01758, 0.17843, -0.00555]`. A camera at `[-3.9, 2.9, -6.8]` shows the back of the panel on the left, close to the supplied reference composition. NASA Eyes’ public model is for visualization. Some mesh names refer to a 2022 assembly. Component-level fidelity to flight configuration needs corroboration from mission-team drawings or photographs, especially for mounts, connectors, and lower surfaces.

## Release review

- Capture frontal and three-quarter views, both panel faces, the underside, and the shield opening under consistent lighting. Check all three rims, layer spacing, panel and attachments, bus silhouette, and visible assemblies. Record a photo or video timestamp for each major feature.
- Run a full turn on desktop and mobile. Check clipping, materials at different lighting angles, hover/focus outlines, label readability, touch, and reduced motion. Show asset provenance and credits in the About section.
- Credit the model to **NASA/JPL-Caltech, NASA Eyes on the Solar System**, and follow the credit for each image. Identify the product using the team’s own branding. NASA guidance generally allows educational and informational use of NASA polygon and texture data. Check third-party terms for each asset before republication.

# Independent Spectral Reference for WISE 0855

The candidate spectrum is JWST/NIRSpec G395M published by **Luhman et al.** in [AJ 167, 5 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ad0b72), using data for Figure 4. The [CDS ReadMe](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/AJ/167/5?format=html&tex=true) lists `fig4.dat` with 1,488 rows: `Wave` in µm, `Flux`, and `e_Flux` in Jy; the two flux columns can be blank. Catalog `J/AJ/167/5`, table `J/AJ/167/5/fig4`, DOI [10.26093/cds/vizier.51670005](https://doi.org/10.26093/cds/vizier.51670005). The paper has a CC BY 4.0 license; table-use conditions must be checked separately before redistributing its data.

The paper reports JWST observation on 2023-04-17 UTC, GTO program 1230, S200A1 slit with G395M/F290LP, three nod positions, and 3,931 seconds total exposure. The spectrum has resolution around 1,000 and useful ranges 2.87–5.09 µm and 5.37–5.54 µm, including SPHEREx D6's 4.4–5.0 µm region. It is a spectrum extracted and combined by the authors from multiple nods, not a single exposure. The team used a custom ESA pipeline, standard stars observed during commissioning, and flux calibration adjusted with multi-epoch [4.5] Spitzer photometry. This spectrum is independent of SPHEREx, but its absolute flux calibration is not independent of the Spitzer calibration used by the authors. The paper identifies the original MAST observations as [10.17909/1fsf-6j64](https://doi.org/10.17909/1fsf-6j64).

**The authors' spectrum file is not available in the workspace.** CDS ReadMe and metadata were checked, but direct `fig4.dat`, VizieR export, and Harvard mirror downloads all timed out from this environment. There is therefore no hash or assumed spectrum data. Retrieval path: open the [CDS catalog](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/VizieR-2?-source=J/AJ/167/5), select table `J/AJ/167/5/fig4`, and export all rows, or use the catalog FTP link for `fig4.dat` specified in the ReadMe. Once accessible, preserve original bytes, retrieval time, URL, SHA-256, and license; verify all 1,488 rows, units, blanks, wavelength ranges, and normalization results. The [CDS registry](https://dc.g-vo.org/rr/q/lp/custom/CDS.VizieR/J/AJ/167/5) update date is 2024-11-06; it does not prove that the file is an immutable snapshot. Machine-readable metadata is in [wise0855-sed-reference.json](../web/data/external/wise0855-sed-reference.json).

A **replacement spectrum from the MAST pipeline** was obtained; it is not the authors' Figure 4 table: [Level 3 x1d product](https://mast.stsci.edu/jwst/api/v0.1/Download/file?uri=mast%3AJWST%2Fproduct%2Fjw01230-o007_t001-s000000001_nirspec_f290lp-g395m-s200a1-subs200a1_x1d.fits), publicly listed by MAST in program 1230 for target WISE0855-0714, F290LP/G395M, dated 2023-04-17. File size 322,560 bytes, SHA-256 `9474fbbcfe1304fa6763fd3a9ea3190f419785763f53a1827ac997aef84e19a6`, CAL_VER `3.0.0`, CRDS `jwst_1584.pmap`, FITS creation time 2026-10-12. MAST products may be reprocessed, so pin this hash before use. The public association file identifies three NRS1 science exposures; the pipeline's extraction/combination differs from Luhman's custom processing and Spitzer scaling. Do not claim the two spectra are equivalent or flux is invariant. [MAST Data Use policy](https://archive.stsci.edu/publishing/data-use) permits public use after the exclusive-access period; citation/attribution details are at that source.

The FITS contains 1,254 rows in EXTRACT1D; `WAVELENGTH` is µm, `FLUX` and `FLUX_ERROR` are Jy, and `DQ` is a bitmask. The conservative technical mask here requires DQ=0, finite flux/error, and positive error: 1,233/1,254 rows pass; 335/335 pass in 4.4–5.0 µm. There are 26 negative flux values among all passing rows; they are retained because noisy measured flux can be negative. This rule does not certify flux accuracy or independence. The [JWST pipeline documentation](https://jwst-pipeline.readthedocs.io/en/1.17.x/jwst/extract_1d/description.html) describes the columns/extraction; NRS1 in slit A can cover this G395M region according to [NIRSpec documentation](https://jwst-docs.stsci.edu/jwst-near-infrared-spectrograph/nirspec-operations/nirspec-fs-operations/nirspec-fs-wavelength-ranges-and-gaps). Do not substitute MAST data for the unavailable author table or apply Luhman's Spitzer factor to this file. Metadata, association, hash, and query provenance are in JSON; the original FITS and responses are under `data/raw/external/wise0855/`. Recheck with `.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/parse_wise0855_x1d.py` and `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m unittest tests.test_wise0855_x1d -v`.

Do not treat `e_Flux` as complete independent uncertainty between wavelengths: the ReadMe supplies no full covariance matrix and absolute calibration has shared components. The spectrum is not simultaneous with SPHEREx images. The paper reports epoch-to-epoch Spitzer scatter when evaluating flux calibration; [Miles et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20664) report wavelength-dependent CO-band variability up to 10% peak-to-peak at some wavelengths during an 11-hour NIRSpec sequence. This is a preprint result, not a variability level applicable to every wavelength or epoch.

This spectrum is not SPHEREx's spectral response. Before a noise-injection study, lock how to integrate the per-pixel spectral response, flux units, PSF, temporal distribution/variability, and gain/noise model; do not replace a band integral with a monochromatic value without showing that it is appropriate. These steps have not been done, and this reference does not confirm WISE astrometric accuracy.

Position information is separate: [Kirkpatrick et al. 2021, Table 5](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/abd107) gives WISE 0855 at mean epoch MJD 57633.39, with parallax 439.0 ± 2.4 mas, μRA −8123.7 ± 1.3 and μDec +673.2 ± 1.3 mas/year. The row reports 19 Spitzer epochs, no WISE epoch, and 46 Gaia stars used for registration. Per-parameter errors in that table do not provide the full covariance needed to propagate to 2025–2026; verify the μRA convention, ephemeris, and covariance before using it as a quantitative position reference. A spectrum does not provide astrometric truth, and the current WISE prior is unchanged.

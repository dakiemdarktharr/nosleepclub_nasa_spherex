# What Did SPHEREx See? — Product Brief

### Product objective

Release a public Vietnamese–English tool where anyone can find a region of sky and see what SPHEREx observed there. The hackathon submission is the first release of a product intended for ongoing use. A newcomer can start from a marker in the sky. An expert can enter RA/Dec, trace each image back to the archive, and review the comparison criteria.

The challenge requires RA/Dec input and chronological playback of SPHEREx images from the same sky region. Test with Barnard’s Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae, and R Draconis. The orbit and night-sky scenes lead to that task. Use the submitted image for the orbit scene’s composition and corroborated flight references for spacecraft geometry. [Challenge notes](CHALLENGE.md) · [HackAthlone challenge page](https://hackathlone.com/challenges/planet-x-and-spherex)

### User journeys and acceptance

| Screen | User action | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Home** | See the title and SPHEREx above Earth’s limb. Earth is a plane with a slowly moving texture. Hovering or focusing Earth or SPHEREx highlights its outline. The spacecraft rotates to show multiple sides. Activate SPHEREx to open **About SPHEREx**, or Earth to **Explore the sky**. | Both labeled actions work from the scene, with keyboard and touch equivalents. A still image and both actions remain available with reduced motion or failed media. The orbit is labeled as an illustration. |
| **About SPHEREx** | Learn what the mission studies, how its shields protect the telescope and how it collects infrared spectral images. Rotate an approved model or inspect verified views. | Component names and captions cite sources. **Explore the sky** leads directly to the observing flow. The media is identified as a model, photograph or animation. |
| **Explore the sky** | Look up from a ground-level night-sky view. Drag or touch to look around, zoom, and select a star-like observation-region marker. Jump to an off-screen region using a target list or RA/Dec input. | A region can be selected from the scene, the list and coordinates. Arrow keys or labeled controls offer a keyboard path. Query center, region name and observation status are available to assistive technology. |
| **Region overview** | Open a marker to see the observed field, pan and zoom, then inspect image count, dates, spectral bands and source. Select **Compare images** to carry the region and frame into the viewer. | The preview states field of view and image bounds, with a cutout or mosaic label as appropriate. Loading, empty results, upstream failure and single-frame cases have explicit states. |
| **Compare images** | Play images by observation time, scrub, step, pause or blink two compatible images. Inspect metadata or download source data. | Back navigation and language changes retain the observing context. A share link restores RA/Dec, field size, release, filters and frame selection whenever those frames remain available. Each frame carries time, detector, band, display scale, quality and source. |

**Bilingual end-to-end flow.** Use a clear page title in each language version. Translate actions, help, tooltips, empty states, dynamic errors, accessible labels, and human-readable metadata. Persist the language preference and update the document’s `lang`. Preserve scientific identifiers, FITS field names, units, and original values in downloads.

**Routes in the repo.** Use `web/index.html` for home, `#about` for the mission and `#night-sky` for the observing view. Keep existing workspace, sample and report deep links in regression checks. Render both home actions before requesting media so visitors on slower networks can proceed.

### Spacecraft, sky and data specifications

- **Exterior fidelity.** Build a component sheet with bilingual names, source, view, known dimensions and confidence. Inspect the shield opening, bus, underside, both sides of the solar panel, rear and oblique angles. Capture at least six views and a full turn of the web asset under neutral lighting. Have a second reviewer check the silhouette, occlusion, placement and materials. Select the science configuration after dust cover ejection. [NASA dust cover release](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/spherex/2025/03/20/nasas-recently-launched-spherex-ejects-dust-cover-from-telescope/)
- **360° asset and media fallback.** Use NASA’s Eyes’ public visualization model, packaged as a GLB in the repo for free rotation. NASA/JPL-Caltech created it to visualize the deployed spacecraft. Component-level fidelity requires separate corroboration from flight photographs or verified CAD. When a device cannot render 3D, use a credited NASA/JPL-Caltech still or exterior clip and keep both home actions available. [NASA’s Eyes · SPHEREx](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_spherex) · [Original glTF asset](https://eyes.nasa.gov/assets/static/models/sc_spherex/spherex.gltf) · [NASA SVS 14966](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14966/) · [JPL image use policy](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/)
- **Ground-level sky.** Default to an illustrative ground view with consistent ICRS directions as the scene rotates. A location-accurate mode requires a specified observer location and UTC instant, a tested RA/Dec-to-altitude/azimuth transform, and an astronomy reference for cross-checking. Request location only after the user activates that mode.
- **Observation markers.** The star shape is an entry point to a survey region. Give the illustrated sky background, target centers, footprints and observed imagery different indications. Distinguish an available image preview, metadata-only coverage and a verified comparison series. Label the unit being counted, such as metadata rows, distinct observations or eligible frames. [Dataset catalog and coverage checks](DATASET_CATALOG.md)
- **Time series.** Use per-observation DR1/QR3 Spectral Images. Check the footprint, valid pixels after masking, shared WCS grid and display scale, observation times, spectral response at the target and processing version. A shared detector label still requires a pixel-level spectral check. Use the DR1 coadded cube as a background or for spectra. Preserve source, observation ID, time and time scale, band and units, mask, version and correctly scoped hash. Label observed data, propagated coordinates, synthetic controls and model outputs distinctly. [IRSA DR1](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/SPHEREx/docs/overview_dr1.html) · [Frame API contract](FRAME_API_CONTRACT.md)

**Integration order.** Agree on region IDs, coordinates, preview states and link encoding first. The UI can complete the path with an attributed sample while the data team qualifies new sequences. The 3D team can supply the attributed still first, then deliver the interactive asset after exterior review. QA tests both paths before rolling out the new scene.

### Release gates and decisions

1. **Real-data journey.** Use RA/Dec and a named target to open the appropriate preview, then play at least three compatible, real frames for one target. Run the same checks on all five challenge targets. Offer A/B when exactly two frames qualify. Explain insufficient data and expose another target. An IRSA failure offers a retry or a pinned, dated, attributed sample.
2. **Bilingual access and a button path.** Exercise both language versions from home to playback. Test mouse, touch, keyboard and screen reader. Verify reduced motion, failed media, offline or poor connectivity, and share-link recovery. A proposed usability gate is four of five newcomers independently opening the correct region within 60 seconds and one experienced user reproducing the view from the link and export.
3. **Operating evidence.** Attach CI results, a preview URL, asset hashes and credits, measured load times on the agreed device/network, and smoke results for the public endpoint. Enforce bounded query size and timeout, the FITS source policy and release-aware caching. If saved reviews are enabled, test session isolation, expiry, deletion and recovery. Publish an operational owner, alert thresholds and rollback instructions. [Existing acceptance criteria](PRODUCTION_READINESS.md) · [Operations runbook](OPERATIONS.md)

These gates are acceptance criteria. The release handoff records which items have evidence and which remain open, tied to a commit or preview.

**Verified locally.** The viewer played three real SPHEREx DR1 D6 images from the Epsilon Indi B field, observed on **29 April, 1 May and 7 May 2025 UTC**. The images share a WCS grid, mask policy and display scale. Their common band covers **99.755%** of the widest band at the field center, estimated from WCS-WAVE. Cutouts took **4.6–17.6 seconds** to load during this run. Barnard’s Star latency varied and needs further measurement. Full-flow checks across all five targets and checks on the public deployment remain pending. The run establishes playback. Resolving the binary or validating motion and variability requires separate scientific evidence. [Observation, mask and hash receipt](data/spherex-dr1-timeline-smoke.json)

**Decisions needed.** Who approves spacecraft fidelity and astronomy comparability? Should the first visit default to EN, VI or the browser locale? Should the first release use the illustrative full-sky view or include location and time? Should the lead target be chosen once a series qualifies or selected in advance? Which components require CAD or dedicated photographic evidence before release? What are the delivery deadline, hosting budget and baseline device?
